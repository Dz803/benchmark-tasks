# Proposed low-voltage glass-case trial

This is a build specification for a bench demonstration, not a tested emergency-light installation. The case has a clear **polycarbonate** lid hinged at its rear edge (the simulated glass look is visual only), a folding U-shaped bail handle on a 60-degree pivot, and a battery-fed LED worklight. No mains wiring is used. The dimensions below match `task.py`; cut tolerance is ±2 mm except the stops and switch trip points. The [dimensioned drawing](build_plan.svg) gives the top and side layout. The [Southco P9 folding grab handle](https://media.southco.com/media/static/Literature/p9.en.pdf) is a commercially available **shape and hinge-spacing reference** (64 mm centres); it is not an electrical switch. This bench mechanism uses 72 mm arms and a separate pivot cam, so it should not be represented as an unmodified P9 product.

| Cabinet | One 180 × 330 mm outside footprint, 6 mm plywood or MDF base and walls; 44 mm base and 174 mm side height. Fix it to the bench. A low-cost cut-sheet cabinet is intentional; this is not claimed to reproduce a certified emergency product. |
| Upward-opening clear lid | One 147 × 150 × 6 mm clear polycarbonate panel with a 102 × 20 × 20 mm grip bar at its front edge. Add two rows of opaque yellow vinyl lettering on its outer face: `BACKUP` up to 115 × 25 mm and `POWER` up to 95 × 25 mm; centre them 40 mm apart so both fit within the panel. A 5 mm rear hinge pin lies 180 mm above the worktop and 75 mm behind the case centre. Use two hinge leaves, washers and a 140° hard stop. The panel rotates upward and back beyond vertical; it does not slide. |
| Folding U-shaped actuating handle | Two 72 mm long black aluminium side arms, centre-to-centre spacing 64 mm, joined by an 80 × 22 × 18 mm red graspable crossbar at 77 mm above the hinge axis. Add a 30 × 3 × 13 mm front grip ridge at the crossbar centre. Use a 5 mm steel pivot pin, two 13 mm radius hubs and washers. A 3 mm aluminium index plate has stops at 0° and 60° on a 22 mm radius; an OFF spring and a resettable one-way pawl hold the ends of travel. Cut both stop slots to ±2° and verify the released ON position stays at least 48°. |
| Electrical trip | One [Adafruit #818 normally-open microswitch](https://www.adafruit.com/product/818) on an adjustable bracket. A small cam fixed to the pivot hub contacts the switch at 48° ±2°. The switch is a **signal input**; it does not carry lamp power. |
| Latch and supply | One [Adafruit #1400 latching DC switch](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1400), one in-stock [Adafruit #4288 5 V, 2.1 A USB battery bank](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4288), one USB-A male breakout lead, one USB-A female breakout lead, insulated low-voltage wire and terminals. Do not use four NiMH cells directly as a nominal 5 V source. |
| Backup worklight | One [Adafruit #5137 5 V USB LED ring](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5137) held about 550 mm above the work area on a metal clamp that also dissipates heat. Keep its inline switch ON; the #1400 controls power upstream of its USB plug. |
| Search light | One approximately 180 mm, 200 g handheld flashlight; the [Streamlight 4AA ProPolymer Lux](https://www.streamlight.com/products/detail/4aa-propolymer-lux-div-1) is a size/form reference, not a required or mechanically identical product. |

The four named electrical parts total about US $44 at the listed prices checked 2026-11-17. Allow roughly US $35–55 for sheet, pin, pawl, stops, fasteners, clamp, two USB breakout leads and wire. Thus this proposed bench fixture is approximately US $80–100 in materials, excluding the flashlight, shipping and tax; no CNC, custom PCB or specialist test gear is required. Prices and availability vary; use the linked part functions and stated dimensions when substituting.

Mount the bail pivot at case-centred X=+18, Y=+18 mm, 78 mm above the table. The closed lid spans Y=−75 to +75 mm. Lift the front grip upward until the lid has rotated at least 115°; the 140° stop prevents overtravel. The hinge geometry leaves the lid's centre of mass behind the pin after it passes vertical, so gravity can hold it open during the handle operation. Verify this stay-open behaviour on the actual build before any robot trial. The 115° opening is the scored minimum before the first pull. The bail rests upright at 0° OFF and swings downward to the 60° ON stop. On a 22 mm-radius index plate, drill a 3 mm hole for a 2 mm spring-loaded pawl pin at the 50° position; provide a 0–60° arcuate clearance slot, a 60° hard stop, and a manual pawl release for reset. The pin must engage at 50° and stop reverse travel below 49° after release; verify those measured angles and change hole position if needed. Set the microswitch cam to first close at 48° ±2°; connect its normally-open leads across the #1400's two button pads, in parallel with the board's own button. The #1400 toggles only once when the cam first closes; the pawl keeps the cam engaged until reset.

Use these low-voltage connections, with the inline switch on the LED ring kept ON:

```text
#4288 USB-A 5 V output -> male USB breakout red (+5 V) -> #1400 IN
#4288 USB-A ground     -> male USB breakout black      -> #1400 GND
#1400 OUT              -> female USB breakout red      -> #5137 USB-A plug
#1400 GND              -> female USB breakout black    -> #5137 USB-A plug
#818 two terminals     -> #1400 button pads (parallel with onboard button)
```

The USB data wires are unused and individually insulated. Verify +5 V/ground polarity and at least 1 A available at the ring before connecting it; the maker specifies a 1 A maximum draw for #5137. Do not connect it to mains. The ring maker recommends heat sinking or reduced drive for operation beyond one minute. The simulated 60-second run limit does not certify the heat or electrical design of a real installation.

For reset, release both grippers, release the pawl and move the bail to its 0° OFF stop, close the lid, turn the lamp latch off, black out the normal room lights, and place the **lit** flashlight in one gripper with its initial beam away from the case. Mark 0.5° and 115° on the lid hinge, and 1.5° and 48° on the bail pivot using a protractor or calibrated side image. A side photo and synchronized video can check the measured angles and continuous illumination from first lid motion through first pull. The simulated 28° aim criterion, unobstructed ray, friction and light intensity are proxies; no lux, switch torque or real-gripper success is claimed. A real trial should verify illumination visually on every video frame from first lid movement through first pull.

In the simulated camera layout, the cabinet's front opening faces the overhead/front view. The held flashlight approaches the front of the cabinet, so its beam enters above the front rim rather than striking a side wall. The rendered beam uses a 34° main cone plus a weaker 70° peripheral spill, with zero room light until the bail trips the backup lamp. The scorer additionally rejects a lamp-to-handle ray blocked by the solid case walls. The simulated intensity is an exposure setting, not a measured lumen value. The cabinet remains illuminated in the recorded contact demo while the lid turns upward and the bail is pulled downward.

The clear lid carries two opaque yellow `BACKUP POWER` lettering rows on its outside. They add no light source. The recorded fixed-layout contact demo starts with the flashlight beam away from the case, searches left to right, first aims at the handle after 109 control steps (5.45 s), and reaches score 1.0 in 498 steps with the lid at about 140° and bail at 60°. A separate Dream-1 beta run on this same task and scorer used seed 42, 320×176 camera input and a 1,200-step limit; it illuminated the control but did not complete the lid-and-bail sequence, scoring 0.2. These results establish one successful scripted trajectory and one model failure in simulation; they do not establish randomized-layout robustness or real-world success.

The scorer monitors illumination at every 20 Hz control step from the lid's first measurable opening at 0.5° through first switch activation, rejects a first handle pull before the lid reaches 115°, and permanently zeroes a trial after a dark opening, early pull or dark activation. Its line-of-sight test excludes the transparent lid but includes opaque case walls. Four synthetic unsafe changes to a measured success trace each score 0; these are scorer controls, not extra physical rollouts. The torch housing and lid grip bar remain visually close in the successful run; no continuous collision-free clearance, measured lux or real-gripper success is claimed.
